Consumer instinct from the real world

Two audiences, one product
At a family-run education company, parents pay and students attend. I design the decision for the payer and the experience for the user — applying motivation theory, because their drivers aren't the same and the same screen can't serve both.
Proves — the decision-maker vs. end-user nuance most products miss
Acquisition by design
Built gifting offers and behavioral nudges that pulled real enrollments in — using loss aversion, default effects, and the endowment effect to make signing up the path of least resistance, not a harder sell.
Proves — applied behavioral design on real customers, not a growth dashboard
Retention that compounds
Shaped the experience with habit-formation and variable-reward research so families don't just stay — they refer. Word-of-mouth from happy parents became the cheapest channel, paying for the next cohort's acquisition.
Proves — retention instinct where churn costs real money
