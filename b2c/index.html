Role
Most social-discovery products either rule-engineer the feed or wait until they have enough behavioral data to train a recommender. I bet on neither — instead a multi-dimensional OpenAI embedding of user context with real-time cosine-similarity ranking. The bet was that semantic match would beat a cold-start recommender at small scale and beat hand-tuned rules everywhere. With 2,500+ live users the embedding rank produced a +22.8% engagement lift versus the rule-only baseline — compounding faster than a behavioral recommender could have at that data volume.

Consumer instinct from the real world

Two audiences, one product
At the family-run education company, parents pay and students attend — same screen has to do two different jobs. I designed the decision for the payer and the experience for the user, applying motivation theory: the drivers aren't the same, the calls-to-action aren't the same, and pretending otherwise is how products lose both sides at once.
Proves — the decision-maker vs. end-user nuance most products miss
Acquisition by design
Built gifting offers and behavioral nudges that pulled real enrollments in — using loss aversion, default effects, and the endowment effect to make signing up the path of least resistance, not a harder sell. Real customers, real revenue, real revealed-preference signal — not a growth dashboard.
Proves — applied behavioral design on revealed-preference data
Retention that compounds
Shaped the experience with habit-formation and variable-reward research so families don't just stay — they refer. Word-of-mouth from happy parents became the cheapest channel, paying for the next cohort's acquisition. Same loop SyncSo runs on: keep the user, and the user becomes acquisition.
Proves — retention instinct where churn costs real money
