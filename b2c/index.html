Game project · Team build
Iterative game design under competition pressure
Built a game with friends inside a competition with over 50 teams. Most teams went one of two ways: over-scoped and shipped something broken, or polished features no real player cared about. We took the third path — ran user interviews from the start of the build, every single week. Sat with real players, watched where they got stuck, where they got bored, where they lit up — and adjusted accordingly. Mechanics, difficulty curves, onboarding flow, all rebuilt against what the feedback actually said versus what we wished it said. The discipline of iterating on revealed-preference signal — not opinion — is what put us above the rest. Result: 2nd place.
2nd of 50+ teams · Iteration won the race
Family ed · Operator
Made them want to stay
My family founded and runs an education company that scaled to thousands of students. As it grew, a long-running safety problem emerged: kids wouldn't wait inside for parent pickup — they ran around outside, in the parking lot and the street. Real liability. The standard moves had all been tried — written rules, classroom reminders from teachers, stricter regulation, scolding when caught — and none of them stuck. Because none of them changed what kids actually wanted to do. Kids are kids; they'll do whatever's most interesting. I proposed the inverse: stop trying to force compliance, design the option they'd choose. We built a small play space at the school — toys, picture books, a TV running familiar shows. Within weeks, kids stayed inside on their own — not because they were told to, but because the option got better than running outside. Cheaper than hiring more security. A multi-year problem dissolved.
Pull, not push · 1000s of students · Multi-year issue solved

How a bet actually gets made

Find the right pain via revealed-preference research, ship the smallest test that earns real behavioral signal — architected before built, refined relentlessly — and measure for revealed preference, not vanity counts. AI runs inside the loop at every stage: deep research at the front, hands-on build acceleration in the middle, structured signal-gathering at the back.
