Context fragmentation
The team needed a production RAG agent on a complex technical knowledge base full of system tables. I made two calls the standard cookbook didn't: Markdown-aware structural splitting that preserved table structure instead of chunking by character count, and a Redis-backed semantic cache for recurring queries. The structural splitter cut context fragmentation by 20%; the cache pulled repeat-query latency under 50ms and materially lowered LLM API spend. Faithfulness landed at 90% on the eval set, and the cost line stayed defensible at scale.

Where my judgment shows up

Two-sided business, not just users
Inside a real, revenue-generating education business I had to hold both sides of the marketplace at once: parents who pay, students who attend, and staff with their own incentive structure. Pricing, discounts, refund logic, and staff comp had to be coherent with each other — anchor prices that made discounts feel meaningful, incentive-compatible commissions that didn't quietly reward the wrong outcome. I learned to think in object models — lead → offer → voucher → redemption → payout — before I learned the words for them.
Proves — operator instinct for commercial-process design, not just engineering
I take the hard note and adjust
A fellow founder pushed back hard: vibe-coding doesn't hold up once you have real users. Instead of defending it I moved to an architecture-first approach and kept shipping — the product now has 2,500+ users on a clean microservices backbone, and that decision compounded for everything after it.
Proves — coachable, judgment over ego, architecture-first when it matters
Pricing designed, not guessed
At the family-run education company I designed packages, discounts, and staff incentives with named frameworks — price anchoring on the top tier, loss aversion on the gift-back offer, incentive-compatible payoffs so the commission structure didn't quietly reward the wrong outcome. Real revenue depended on getting it right. AI pipelines replaced manual operations; the pricing structure determined which ones got built first.
Proves — mechanism-design instinct on real money, not a textbook
